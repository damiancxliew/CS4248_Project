\subsection{Sentence Ranker and Joint Decoding}
\label{sec:sentence-ranker}

The evidence model adds a sentence-ranking head to the span extractor
(\S\ref{sec:span-extractor}), sharing its encoder. For each sentence $j$ visible in a window, the
head mean-pools the sentence's token states into $\bar{\mathbf{h}}_j$ and scores it with one linear
layer, $s_j=\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathrm{dropout}(\bar{\mathbf{h}}_j)+b$.

\paragraph{Training.} Two losses train the head on each window that shows the gold sentence $g$
(\S\ref{sec:evidence-labels}). A listwise cross-entropy over the window's sentences,
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{rank}}=-\log\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{s})_g$, ranks $g$ first. A margin
loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{neg}}=\max(0,\,m-s_g+s_n)$ pushes $g$ above one negative sentence $n$. By
default $n$ is mined online: among the window's candidates (visible, not gold, and containing no
reference answer string), the one the head currently scores highest, so the negative follows the
model's own confusions as it trains. The ablations replace it with a fixed BM25-selected
(\emph{lexical}) or random candidate, or drop $\mathcal{L}_{\text{neg}}$. The objective is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\text{start}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{end}})
+\alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{rank}}+\beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{neg}}.
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Ranking and joint decoding.} At inference, each window's sentence scores are
log-softmaxed over its visible sentences, and a sentence's passage-level log-probability
$\log p(j)$ is its maximum over the windows that show it; sorting by $\log p(j)$ gives the
passage ranking. Joint decoding rescores every candidate span $(a,b)$ of the standard decoder,
with start and end logits $z^{\text{start}}_a$ and $z^{\text{end}}_b$, as
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{score}(a,b)=z^{\text{start}}_a+z^{\text{end}}_b+\lambda\log p\bigl(\mathrm{sent}(a)\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where $\mathrm{sent}(a)$ is the sentence holding the span's start. Setting $\lambda=0$ recovers
span-only decoding exactly. We tune $\alpha$, $\beta$, $m$ and $\lambda$ on val
(\S\ref{sec:ablations}).
